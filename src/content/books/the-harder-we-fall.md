  <p>“Do you…” There’s a short pause before he clears his throat. “Do you have a problem with people using your meditation app for… other purposes?”</p>
  <p>Something in his tone draws my attention. He looks so guilty. Oh my flippity goodness, has Tristan been getting off on my voice? “Between us,” I admit, “I think it’s kind of awesome, even if I don’t really get it.”</p>
  <p>“I get it.” He shifts in his chair, looking generally uncomfortable with the topic of conversation. “I’ve only been using the app for a few weeks but… it’s saved my life.” Though he’s not looking at me, the intensity of his tone gives weight to his words. “The sound of your voice, it’s like a shot of whiskey to my system. My whole body gives in to it and I can’t help but—” He stalls, as if realising he’s said too much.</p>
  <p>“Don’t stop there,” I murmur, low and teasing. “I’d love to know what you can’t help but do.”</p>
  <p>Both hands run through his hair as he shifts again. “I think you get the idea.”</p>
  <p>I break out into a grin. “What are you, gay or something?”</p>
  <p>“Yes,” he confirms without hesitation.</p>
  <p>My insides do a happy dance, even as my brain urges caution. It’s entirely possible Tristan’s attraction to me begins and ends with my voice. I don’t want to let my imagination run away with me only to discover his interest is limited to my vocal cords. “It must have been a relief to meet me in person,” I suggest, “and realise there’s nothing siren-ish about me, after all. Not in the flesh.”</p>
  <p>Tristan’s gaze flicks over me before dropping away again. “You underestimate your flesh.” His words are thick and heavy with desire.</p>
  <p>Shivers of pleasure rush through me and my eyes slide closed briefly. “Well, I’m also gay,” I say, trying not to sound like I’m thrusting the information in his face. Which, okay, I am, but I don’t want to be obvious about it. “The thought of a gorgeous man finding pleasure in my voice is never going to be a bad thing. As for the flesh…” I allow the words to trail away as our eyes meet.</p>
  <p>Tristan’s throat moves as he swallows. “Sam, I can’t,” he rasps. “We’re working together.”</p>
  <p>Is that what’s holding him back? “We have a blood ink pact,” I correct him. “It’s entirely more intimate.”</p>
  <p>Huffing out a laugh, he tilts his head to one side. “You’re mouthy tonight.”</p>
  <p>“I know, sorry about that.” I don’t sound sorry because I’m not really. “I get this way when I start feeling comfortable with someone.”</p>
  <p>His eyebrows lift. “You feel comfortable with me?”</p>
  <p>“I do. Not with the work you’re giving me.” I gesture to the nearby task list. “But with you, yes. I’m glad we met.”</p>
  <p>He smiles the shadow smile. The reluctant one that sits so oddly upon his lips. “I’m glad, too.”</p>

Insomnia is the penance she pays for her greatest mistake — until Sleep with Me, a locally-made meditation app, promises a cure, and its creator's voice starts getting inside her head every night.

Meeting **Sam** in person only complicates things further: he's quiet, reclusive, and impossible to resist. She has no right to love, not after the damage she's done. But Sam refuses to let her guilt be the only thing standing between them.

*Trigger Warning: This book deals with issues of long-term complicated grief over the death of a loved one.*
