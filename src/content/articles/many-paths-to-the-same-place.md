<p>After finishing nine books and starting on my tenth, I've reached one conclusion about the writing process: every book is different. This may not sound like a big deal, but for me it's actually quite horrifying.<br><br>When I first started writing, I spent a lot of time reading about the wonderful and unique writing processes of other authors. I always imagined that after writing a few books I would discover my own process and that would be that. I would be able to use that same process to write book after book, streamlining and refining as I went.<br><br>The reality? <em>Splinter</em> took forever to write, <em>Our Little Secret</em> was done in a few short months. Writing <em>All the Broken Pieces</em> hurt my heart, but the constant rewrites for <em>Becoming Us</em> hurt my brain. <em>The Experiment</em> plotted itself practically before I put pen to paper. The new book I'm writing started with a spark of an idea for one facet of one character and I had to consciously build the story from there.<br><br>Plotting. Characterisation. Relationship arcs. Nothing ever seems to work the same way twice! It's enough to send a pragmatist bonkers.<br><br>Part of me still hopes that one day I will stumble across a tried and true path to follow when writing. But the creative part of me loves using a new approach every time - even if my muse is forcing me to do it. Either way, I least I know I'll never (ever, ever, ever) get bored.</p>
